Skyrim Anniversary Edition PS4 “True 100%” Guide Specification

1. Project Purpose

Create a written, linear, black-box completion guide for The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim Anniversary Edition on PS4, played on Legendary difficulty, with PlayStation trophies preserved.

The guide is not a full walkthrough. It should tell the player what to do next, when to delay content, when to hard save, when to update the completion checklist, and when to avoid irreversible mistakes. It should not explain every dungeon, puzzle, dialogue option, combat encounter, or story event unless necessary to prevent a missable, conflict, bug, or permanently inferior reward.

The guide should be suitable for a maximalist completionist run: one main continuity completing as much finite official trophy-safe content as possible, with hard-save branches for mutually exclusive content that cannot coexist in a single final save.

2. Platform and Content Constraints

2.1 Platform

* Game: The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim Anniversary Edition / Skyrim Special Edition with Anniversary Upgrade.
* Platform: PS4.
* Difficulty: Legendary.
* Trophy preservation: mandatory.

2.2 Allowed Content

The guide may use only:

* Base game content.
* Official Dawnguard, Hearthfire, and Dragonborn content.
* Official Anniversary Edition / Anniversary Upgrade Creation Club bundle content.

2.3 Forbidden Content

The guide must exclude:

* Non-AE mods.
* Modern Creations not included in the official AE bundle.
* Paid or free third-party Creations outside the AE entitlement.
* Any content that disables trophies on PS4.
* Cut content, console-command-only content, PC-only mods, and exploit-only objectives unless mentioned in a clearly optional appendix.

2.4 Setup Rule

The first section of the guide must instruct the player to:

1. Confirm the Anniversary Edition / Upgrade entitlement.
2. Download/install all owned official AE Creation Club content.
3. Avoid installing any other Creations or mods.
4. Avoid adding/removing content mid-run unless explicitly confirmed trophy-safe.

3. Completion Scope

The chosen scope is maximalist, including all perks.

The guide should include every finite, trophy-safe, official objective that a serious Skyrim AE completionist could reasonably care about, unless the objective is impossible, infinite, purely arbitrary, or mutually exclusive with the chosen main continuity.

3.1 Mandatory Main-Route Scope

The main route must include:

* All PS4 trophies.
* Main quest.
* Civil War questline, routed safely around trophy and main-quest interactions.
* Companions questline.
* College of Winterhold questline.
* Thieves Guild questline, including restoration requirements and bounded radiant jobs required for completion.
* Dark Brotherhood questline on the main save.
* Bard’s College questline and associated instruments/objectives.
* Dawnguard main quest and finite side content on one canonical main-route faction.
* Hearthfire land acquisition, house building, furnishing, adoption, and related trophies.
* Dragonborn main quest and finite side content.
* All official AE Creation Club quests, systems, locations, homes, pets, items, spells, ingredients, and rewards.
* All named non-radiant side quests.
* All finite miscellaneous quests and favors that are reasonably trackable.
* Bounded radiant content required for trophies, faction restoration, unique rewards, unlocks, or representative completion.
* All Daedric quests, routed to preserve artifacts and Oblivion Walker.
* All dragon shouts and word walls.
* Sufficient dragon souls to unlock all shouts.
* All map-marked locations discovered.
* All clearable locations cleared, where clearing is possible.
* All finite collectible sets.
* All unique items obtainable on the main route.
* Best-tier versions of leveled unique rewards wherever possible.
* All player homes, including city homes, Hearthfire homes, AE homes, farm, and relevant upgrades/furnishings.
* All pets and mounts obtainable on the main route.
* All thaneships, housecarls, faction ranks, titles, and major relationship unlocks.
* All permanent spells, powers, abilities, transformations, and Black Book powers obtainable on the main route.
* All skills to 100.
* All perks acquired.
* All enchantments learned, except where learning an enchantment would destroy an irreplaceable unique item that the guide chooses to preserve.
* All alchemy ingredient effects discovered.
* All merchant investments, if available under the chosen perk path and NPC survival conditions.
* All practical crafting-system unlocks and recipe/system knowledge tracked by the chosen checklist.

3.2 Branch-Save Scope

Where content is mutually exclusive, the guide should prescribe hard saves and branch instructions.

Branches should cover at least:

* Civil War side choice, unless one side is selected as canonical and the other is only documented in an appendix.
* Dawnguard vs Volkihar faction choice.
* Dark Brotherhood join vs destroy.
* Paarthurnax / Blades conflict.
* Daedric quests with mutually exclusive rewards or outcomes.
* Any AE quest with meaningful mutually exclusive outcomes.
* Marriage/steward choices, if the guide chooses to document alternatives rather than require only one canonical assignment.

Branch content should be treated as “experienced via hard save,” not as part of the single final world state.

3.3 Exclusions

The guide should exclude from required completion:

* Infinite repetition of radiant quests.
* Arbitrary repeated Legendary skill resets beyond what is necessary to acquire all perks.
* Every possible random encounter variant.
* Every copy of every book.
* Every possible spouse, steward, or adoption configuration in one save.
* Arbitrary crime-stat maximization.
* Arbitrary kill counts.
* Arbitrary wealth accumulation beyond practical completion needs.
* Exploit-only duplicate rewards unless explicitly chosen as optional.
* Non-trophy-safe content.

4. Relationship to the 100% Completion Checklist Spreadsheet

The guide must be designed to work alongside the 100% completion checklist spreadsheet.

4.1 Coverage Requirement

The guide should include at least every objective tracked in the spreadsheet, plus any additional maximalist objectives selected for this project.

4.2 Checklist Synchronization

Whenever a routed instruction completes or advances an objective that appears in the spreadsheet, the guide should explicitly mark it with a checklist cue.

Possible notation:

* [CHECKLIST: Quest]
* [CHECKLIST: Location]
* [CHECKLIST: Unique Item]
* [CHECKLIST: Skill Book]
* [CHECKLIST: AE Creation]
* [CHECKLIST: Trophy]
* [CHECKLIST: Property]
* [CHECKLIST: Spell/Power]
* [CHECKLIST: Enchantment]
* [CHECKLIST: Alchemy]
* [CHECKLIST: Collectible]

The guide should not require the player to remember that an earlier instruction corresponds to the spreadsheet. The update cue should appear inline at the point of completion or acquisition.

4.3 Checklist-First Validation

Before finalizing the actual route, the guide writer should map every checklist row/objective to one or more route steps or appendices.

No checklist objective should be left unmapped unless explicitly excluded and justified.

4.4 Final Main Guide Self-Containment

For the final main guide draft, mapping to appendices is not enough for execution-critical route content. The guide document itself must explicitly represent every objective row in `data/objectives/objectives.csv` and every checklist-relevant row already mapped by the project.

A player following the guide should not need to consult the objective spreadsheet, route-planning CSVs, source notes, support tables, or external appendices to know what to do next. A reviewer with the spreadsheet should be able to find each row directly in the guide by objective/checklist ID, objective/checklist name, or an explicit exclusion/option/branch note.

The guide may include internal reference or verification sections, but core route instructions must be inline at the relevant route point. Category placeholders such as "route local objectives," "collect local books," "finish remaining checklist," "as needed," "selected sources," "support rows," "route locations," or "spell sources" are not acceptable unless immediately followed by the complete list of objective names and concrete actions covered.

If a row cannot be routed from existing project data, the guide should include a `NEEDS ROUTE RESOLUTION:` note that names the objective/checklist ID, the row name, the exact missing fact, and the inputs already checked.

5. Core Form: Linear Black-Box Itinerary

The guide should be a linear sequence of numbered instructions, divided into meaningful sections.

The reader should be able to proceed from top to bottom without needing to remember delayed tasks from earlier sections.

5.1 No Implicit Backtracking

The guide must not say things like:

* “Come back here later after level 40,” unless the guide later gives an explicit return instruction.
* “Remember to finish this after joining X,” unless the later section explicitly commands it.
* “Save this quest for later,” without routing the player back to it later.

Instead, the guide should either:

1. Delay sending the player there until the content is ready to complete; or
2. Send the player there early only for safe partial completion, then later include an explicit instruction such as:
    * “Return to [place] now that you are level 46 and complete [quest].”

5.2 Section Structure

Sections should be meaningful but subordinate to the linear route. Possible section types:

* Setup and global rules.
* Early survival and character stabilization.
* Pre-dragon NPC safety sweep.
* Hold-by-hold local questing.
* Early faction introductions.
* Property and economy establishment.
* Midgame faction development.
* Controlled crafting and skill progression milestones.
* Daedric/artifact phase.
* Civil War and main-quest coordination.
* Dawnguard phase.
* High-level AE phase.
* Dragonborn/Solstheim phase.
* Late-game leveled reward acquisition.
* Max skills / all perks / Legendary grind phase.
* Final map/location/collectible cleanup.
* Branch-save appendices.

5.3 Instruction Granularity

The default instruction should be compact:

* “Go to [location]. Start/complete [quest].”
* “While here, acquire [specific tracked item/objective].”
* “Do not advance [quest] past [safe stopping point].”
* “Hard save before [choice]. Choose the option that awards [artifact/reward].”
* “Update checklist entries: [objective categories].”

The guide should not normally include:

* Full puzzle solutions.
* Exact dungeon room-by-room routing.
* Dialogue transcripts.
* Enemy-by-enemy tactics.
* Plot summaries.
* Generic loot instructions.

Exceptions are allowed only when needed to prevent a missable, bug, trophy failure, or permanently inferior reward.

6. Information Policy

6.1 Always Include Inline

For each routed objective, include enough of the following to make the step safe and checklist-synchronized:

* Objective name.
* Location or start trigger.
* Required prerequisite.
* Recommended level, if important.
* Hard level gate, if important.
* Completion boundary.
* Checklist cue.
* Trophy cue, if relevant.
* Unique reward cue, if relevant.
* Save instruction, if relevant.
* Delay warning, if relevant.
* “Do not enter before level X” warning, if relevant.
* Spoiler-light choice instruction, if relevant.

6.2 Include Only as Warnings

Use minimal but explicit warnings for:

* Daedric choices.
* Dawnguard faction choice.
* Dark Brotherhood join/destroy branch.
* Paarthurnax/Blades conflict.
* Civil War / Season Unending interactions.
* War Hero risk.
* NPC death risks.
* Leveled reward thresholds.
* Cell-entry locking.
* Bug-prone quests.
* Unique item disenchanting/selling risks.
* AE difficulty spikes.

6.3 Put in Appendices or Tables

Use appendices for exhaustive reference data, including:

* Full unique item checklist.
* Full leveled item table.
* Full AE Creation start-condition table.
* Full location checklist.
* Skill books.
* Stones of Barenziah.
* Crimson Nirnroot.
* Dragon priest masks.
* Claws.
* Treasure maps.
* Bugs in jars.
* Paragons.
* Black Books.
* Fishing species and fishing objectives.
* Pets and mounts.
* Followers, spouses, stewards, housecarls.
* Homes and upgrades.
* Enchantments.
* Alchemy effects.
* Spells.
* Merchant investments.
* Radiant quest boundaries.
* Survival Mode modifications.
* Branch-save routes.

However, the main route must still explicitly tell the player when to perform appendix-backed objectives. Appendices are for verification, not for remembering deferred tasks.

7. Ordering and Routing Principles

The guide should route objectives according to the following priority order.

7.1 Hard Constraints First

Mandatory constraints override all aesthetic or pacing concerns:

1. Trophy safety.
2. Avoiding non-AE content.
3. Avoiding quest failure or permanent missables.
4. Avoiding permanently inferior leveled unique rewards.
5. Avoiding cell-entry locking for leveled items.
6. Preserving NPCs needed for quests, favors, training, marriage, investment, or property.
7. Avoiding known major bugs.
8. Maintaining access to all required properties, titles, factions, and collectibles.

7.2 Level-Gated Reward Routing

The route must delay leveled rewards until their maximum useful tier.

Known high-priority gates include:

* Mage’s Circlet: level 25+.
* The Pale Blade: level 27+.
* Nightingale Armor: level 32+.
* Gauldur Blackblade / Gauldur Blackbow: level 36+.
* Shield of Solitude: level 40+.
* Chillrend: level 46+.
* Dragonbane: level 46+.
* Nightingale Blade: level 46+.
* Nightingale Bow: level 46+.
* Miraak’s Sword and Miraak’s mask: level 60+.

The actual guide must verify all leveled items and their locking conditions before final routing.

7.3 No Early Cell Entry for Sensitive Items

If an item may lock on cell entry rather than pickup, the guide must either:

* Avoid routing the player to that location before the required level; or
* Explicitly warn: “Do not enter [location] before level X.”

7.4 Legendary Difficulty Pacing

The route must avoid making the player overleveled but underpowered, while also avoiding early over-optimization that would collapse the intended difficulty curve.

The guide should ensure the player maintains:

* At least one reliable offensive skill.
* At least one defensive skill or mitigation path.
* A sustain option such as restoration, alchemy, enchanting, summons, followers, or strong consumables.
* Gear quality appropriate to the current level.

The guide should not front-load non-combat skill leveling without also improving combat power. It should also not rush best-in-slot crafting, maximum-strength enchantments, or broken power loops early unless doing so is necessary to prevent unreasonable Legendary-difficulty friction.

7.5 Even Progression Across Systems

The guide should distribute progression across major systems rather than completing one entire progression path absurdly early unless there is a strong reason.

The guide should interleave:

* Quest progression.
* Holds and local content.
* Faction arcs.
* Property acquisition.
* Crafting development.
* Skill leveling.
* Collectibles.
* AE content.
* DLC content.
* Location clearing.

Exceptions are allowed where early completion is optimal, such as obtaining the Stones of Barenziah reward early enough to benefit from it.

7.6 Lore / Order-of-Gravity Pacing

Where hard constraints allow, the route should escalate in narrative gravity:

1. Local survival, mundane favors, and early hold content.
2. Thaneships, property, and local reputation.
3. Early guild involvement.
4. Wider faction arcs and regional threats.
5. Political conflict and Civil War.
6. Daedric and supernatural content.
7. Main quest climax.
8. Dawnguard-level ancient/apocalyptic threats.
9. Dragonborn/Solstheim and high-level mythic content.
10. Late AE threats and final completion cleanup.

Hard constraints, level gates, and missable prevention override lore order.

7.7 AE Content Integration

AE content should not be dumped into one separate block. It should be integrated into the main route by:

* Start trigger.
* Location/region.
* Difficulty.
* Level gate.
* Reward relevance.
* Lore gravity.
* Checklist category.

Early low-risk AE systems may appear early. High-level AE questlines should be delayed.

7.8 Cleanup Philosophy

The guide should avoid massive late-game cleanup where possible. Collectibles and local objectives should be collected during natural visits.

However, cleanup sections are acceptable for:

* Objectives inaccessible until late.
* Objectives intentionally delayed for leveled rewards.
* Final skill/perk grinding.
* Final location clearing.
* Final checklist reconciliation.

8. Save and Branch Policy

8.1 General Save Hygiene

The guide must instruct the player to keep:

* Rotating manual saves.
* Named hard saves before irreversible decisions.
* Optional PS cloud or USB backups.

The player must not rely only on autosaves or quicksaves.

8.2 Mandatory Hard Saves

The guide should prescribe named hard saves before:

* Character setup completion, if desired.
* Installing or changing content.
* Major faction choices.
* Dawnguard faction choice.
* Dark Brotherhood join/destroy decision.
* Civil War side commitment, if branch-preserved.
* Season Unending.
* War Hero-sensitive Civil War stages.
* Paarthurnax/Blades decision.
* Daedric final choices.
* Bug-prone quests, especially Blood on the Ice.
* Leveled reward pickups or sensitive cell entries.
* Any AE quest with irreversible choices.
* Marriage, adoption, steward assignment, if alternatives are branch-documented.
* Final checklist completion, before any optional branch/exploit/grind decisions.

8.3 Branch Documentation

Branch handling should follow a selective branch-routing policy.

The guide should fully route only branches with substantial unique content, rewards, trophies, faction experiences, or completionist significance. Smaller mutually exclusive variations may be noted without full routing.

Where a mutually exclusive choice can be isolated and does not materially affect the rest of the route, the guide should not create a branch route. Instead, it should present the available options and let the reader choose. This applies to choices such as spouse, children, steward, decoration, home preference, non-consequential rewards, and similar cases where the route can continue unchanged afterward.

Branch routes should be played before continuing the main route from the same decision point. The guide should therefore use this order:

1. Create named hard save before the irreversible choice.
2. Play the alternate branch first, focusing only on content unique to that branch.
3. Do not perform objectives in the branch that are common to the main route unless required to reach the branch-exclusive content.
4. Record/check off branch-only content according to the guide’s checklist policy.
5. Reload the named hard save.
6. Continue the canonical main route.

Possible format:

* HARD SAVE: create the decision-point save.
* BRANCH ROUTE: play the alternate content first.
* RETURN: reload the hard save.
* MAIN ROUTE: make the canonical choice and continue.

Branch routes must be sharply scoped. They should not duplicate common quests, collectibles, locations, leveling, crafting, or cleanup that will be handled later by the main route. They should include only the alternate faction/choice content, unique rewards, unique trophies if applicable, alternate scenes/objectives, and any branch-exclusive checklist items.

The guide must state whether branch content should be checked off as “experienced via branch” or left unchecked on the main-save checklist.

9. Level Checks, Progression Gates, Grinding, and Exploits

The guide should include explicit level checkpoints.

9.1 Minimum Level Checks

At major route checkpoints, the guide should state a minimum expected level. If the player is below that level, the guide should prescribe a bounded leveling block rather than leaving the player to improvise.

Example:

* “Checkpoint: before starting this section, be at least level 32. If not, complete the next local-location clearing block before proceeding.”

9.2 Hard Level Gates

For leveled rewards, the guide must use hard gates.

Example:

* “Do not complete this quest or enter this location until level 46.”

9.3 All-Perks Requirement

Because the chosen scope includes all perks, the guide must include a plan for acquiring enough perk points.

The guide should distinguish:

* Natural leveling through completion.
* Planned skill leveling to 100.
* Legendary skill resets required to acquire remaining perks.
* Any recommended repeatable skill loop.

The guide should avoid requiring arbitrary infinite Legendary resets beyond the number needed for all perks.

Grinding should be distributed throughout the guide at convenient route points rather than dumped into one enormous endgame block. Grinding blocks should respect Legendary difficulty: leveling non-combat skills must be paired with appropriate combat development, gear improvement, defensive capability, or survivability.

9.4 Grinding and Exploit Policy

Exploits are allowed only when they avoid substantially worse grinding and do not compromise trophy safety, save integrity, or the intended completion scope.

The guide should prefer:

1. Natural progression through routed content.
2. Efficient but legitimate training/crafting/combat loops.
3. Small distributed grind blocks at convenient points.
4. Exploits only when the alternative would be excessive, repetitive, or guide-warping grind.

Exploits should not be used merely to become overpowered early, bypass meaningful content, trivialize the intended difficulty curve, or replace ordinary play.

If an exploit is used, the guide should:

* State why it is being used.
* State what worse grind it avoids.
* Confirm it is trophy-safe on PS4 AE.
* Keep it bounded to the minimum needed.
* Prefer placing it at a natural downtime or progression checkpoint.
* Avoid using it before the player’s combat power can absorb the resulting level gains.

10. Crafting, Gear, and Power Curve Policy

Crafting should be integrated evenly and should preserve a gradually increasing difficulty curve. The guide should avoid rushing to overpowered endgame crafting early, even when doing so would make Legendary difficulty easier. The goal is to keep combat manageable but not flatten the intended escalation of danger.

10.1 Crafting Milestones

The guide should prescribe crafting milestones rather than front-loading all crafting.

Possible milestones:

* Early: basic survivability gear, potions, and money-making.
* Early-mid: first reliable improved gear set.
* Midgame: enchantment and smithing upgrades sufficient for faction and regional content.
* Late-mid: stronger crafted gear for high-level AE/DLC content.
* Late: near-optimal gear.
* Endgame: best armor/upgrades/enchantments/potions.

10.2 Unique Item Preservation

The guide should globally instruct:

* Do not sell unique named items unless the guide explicitly says it is safe.
* Do not disenchant unique named items unless the guide explicitly says that enchantment learning is prioritized over item preservation.
* Store unique items in safe storage.

10.3 Enchanting Conflict

Unique item preservation overrides destructive enchantment learning.

The guide should learn all enchantments available from non-unique, replaceable, or safely disposable items. It should not require disenchanting unique named items, one-of-a-kind artifacts, or irreplaceable quest rewards merely to learn an enchantment.

If the completion checklist distinguishes “all enchantments learned,” the guide should define this as all non-destructively learnable enchantments unless the user later chooses to add an optional destructive branch appendix.

11. Survival Mode Policy

Survival Mode is required for the main route.

The guide should treat Survival Mode as part of the intended Anniversary Edition experience, not as an optional modifier. Routing must therefore account for hunger, fatigue, cold, reduced carry capacity, sleep requirements, travel friction, and the absence or limitation of fast travel.

The route should:

* Group objectives geographically to reduce unnecessary travel.
* Prioritize early access to food, warmth, camping supplies, inns, carriages, ferries, and safe storage.
* Prioritize early homes or bases of operation where sensible.
* Avoid sending the player deep into cold northern regions before adequate preparation.
* Include restock/rest/sleep instructions at major route transitions.
* Treat cold-region and mountain travel as meaningful route constraints.
* Integrate cooking, camping, backpacks, horses, pets, homes, and survival-relevant AE systems into the main progression.
* Avoid assuming normal fast-travel routing.

Survival Mode should shape the guide’s geography-first structure and should be considered in every major routing decision.

12. Radiant and Repeatable Quest Policy

Radiant and repeatable quests must be bounded, but the guide should include one representative completion of each meaningful radiant type.

Include:

* Radiants required for trophies.
* Radiants required for faction restoration.
* Radiants required for unique items, followers, homes, titles, or unlocks.
* Radiant chains with finite endpoints.
* One representative completion of each meaningful radiant type.

Exclude:

* Infinite repetition for its own sake.
* Randomly generated tasks after all unique rewards/unlocks are exhausted.
* Exhaustive variant hunting where variants are mechanically identical and not tracked in a meaningful way.

The guide should clearly mark when a radiant objective is being done because it is required versus when it is being done as representative completion.

13. Books and Documents Policy

Mandatory:

* All skill books.
* All spell tomes needed to learn all spells.
* All Black Books.
* Quest books, notes, and journals required for quests or AE starts.
* Unique books/notes tracked by the checklist.

The guide should not require owning one copy of every readable book title unless that book is already part of the chosen checklist, a quest, a spell/power system, a skill-book set, a Black Book set, or an AE/unique collectible set.

The guide should not attempt to collect every transient physical note/letter/journal unless it is checklist-tracked or necessary for a quest/objective.

Optional appendices may list broader library-completion possibilities, but they are outside the required main-route scope.

14. Marriage, Steward, Adoption, and Role Assignment Policy

The guide should include:

* Marriage completion if in scope.
* Adoption completion for Hearthfire trophies and household completion.
* Housecarl unlocks through thaneships.
* Stewards, bards, carriage drivers, and farmhands where applicable.
* Pets and mounts.

Where a choice is mutually exclusive but does not materially affect the rest of the route, the guide should isolate the choice rather than branch it. The guide should provide a list of valid options, explain any relevant mechanical differences, and let the reader choose.

This applies especially to:

* Spouse selection.
* Adopted children, unless a specific child has route consequences.
* Steward assignments.
* House decoration style choices.
* Non-consequential pet/home/display preferences.
* Similar “choose one from many” decisions that do not affect trophies, quest access, unique rewards, routing, or later checklist completion.

The guide should branch only when the choice creates materially different content, rewards, trophies, faction paths, world states, or later route consequences.

Default-choice policy:

* The guide writer should recommend defaults for spouse, adopted children, stewards, and main base after researching mechanical convenience, route efficiency, Survival Mode usefulness, safety, storage/display value, and thematic fit.
* These defaults should be recommendations, not requirements, unless a particular choice materially affects routing or completion.
* The guide should still present the available options when the choice is isolated and non-consequential.

15. Main Route Canonical Choices

The guide needs canonical main-route choices for mutually exclusive content. These can be overridden by branch saves, but the main route must choose one path to stay linear.

Resolved canonical choices:

* Civil War: Imperial main route.
* Dawnguard: Dawnguard main route.
* Dark Brotherhood: join on the main route.
* Paarthurnax: preserve him on the main route.
* Daedric choices: choose artifact-maximizing route by default.
* Survival Mode: on for the main route.

Resolved branch policy:

* Use selective branch routing.
* Fully route only branches with substantial unique content, rewards, trophies, faction experiences, or completionist significance.
* Play side branches first from the hard save, then reload and continue the canonical main route.
* Branch guides should focus sharply on alternate content and should not duplicate objectives common to the main route.
* Do not branch isolated preference choices that do not materially affect the rest of the route. Present the options, explain relevant differences, and let the reader choose.

Fully route by default:

* Volkihar branch.
* Destroy the Dark Brotherhood.
* Stormcloak Civil War route, if it has enough genuinely distinct content to justify routing.
* Blades/Paarthurnax alternative.
* Major Daedric alternate rewards/outcomes.
* Any AE Creation with mutually exclusive unique rewards or substantial alternate content.

Note/list only by default:

* Minor dialogue variants.
* Cosmetic faction differences with no meaningful unique reward.
* Spouse options.
* Steward options.
* Adopted-child options, unless a specific choice affects route access.
* House decoration or display preferences.
* Other isolated preference choices whose consequences do not propagate through the route.

Default-choice policy:

* The guide writer should recommend default Black Book powers after researching mechanical usefulness, Legendary difficulty value, and route synergy.
* The guide writer should recommend a final transformation state after accounting for trophies, Werewolf/Vampire Lord perk completion, faction choices, roleplay coherence, and mechanical convenience.
* The guide writer should recommend a main home/base of operations after accounting for Survival Mode travel, storage safety, crafting access, display space, family/pet compatibility, and route geography.

These defaults should be presented as recommended choices unless a choice materially affects completion or routing.

16. Proposed Guide Step Template

A route step may use this compact schema:

[Step Number]. [Action instruction].
Location/Start: [where/how]
Requirements: [level/prereq, if relevant]
Warnings: [only if needed]
Rewards/Checklist: [checklist cues]
Save: [none / manual / named hard save]
Stop Point: [if not completing now]

For normal steps, the schema can be compressed into prose:

37. Go to Whiterun. Complete [Quest Name]. Acquire [Item]. [CHECKLIST: Quest, Unique Item]

For dangerous steps:

112. HARD SAVE: DAEDRIC—before final choice in [Quest Name]. Finish the quest and choose the option that awards [Artifact]. Keep the artifact. [CHECKLIST: Daedric Artifact] [TROPHY: Oblivion Walker progress]

For delayed return:

198. You should now be level 46+. Return to [Location] and complete [Quest Name] to acquire the maximum-tier [Reward]. [CHECKLIST: Unique Item]

Most high-level user decisions are now resolved. The guide writer must still research and recommend defaults for choices where the user wants a recommended option but does not have a preference.

Writer-researched default recommendations needed:

2. Adopted children.
